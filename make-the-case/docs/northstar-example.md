# Northstar: a coordinator develops a better client-update process

All people, company details, evidence, timing, and results in this example are fictional. The scenario demonstrates preparation and decision making; no live test or financial benefit is claimed.

Jamie, a client services coordinator, sees three opportunities: weekly update preparation, repeated support questions, and conflicting account-status reports. The team goal is accurate, timely client communication without overtime. Jamie can describe the work and collect timing evidence but does not control IT access or own the account teams.

## Why one idea is the focus

The update workflow has a recurring output, a recognizable reviewer, and a bounded place to investigate. The reporting idea depends on shared definitions; ordinary reporting may be a better starting point. Support routing needs evidence of frequency and failure. All three are retained with these reasons.

## Proposed work

An approved AI tool could prepare a draft from permitted account sources. A coordinator would check facts and missing commitments, and the account lead would approve and send it. The first scope excludes automatic sends, new commitments, CRM write access, and staffing changes.

Jamie proposes a four-week discovery/test commitment with two coordinators and an account lead, conditional on tool/data review, accepted ownership, time, and budget. Operations would help measure; IT/security would confirm access; L&D could support a short checking checklist.

## Working assumptions

| Input | Illustrative assumption |
|---|---:|
| Eligible updates/month | 80 |
| Coverage | 80% |
| Uptake within covered work | 75% |
| Current preparation minutes/update | 22 |
| New draft/processing minutes/update | 6 |
| Check and correct minutes/update | 8 |
| Ongoing support hours/month | 2 |
| Recurring cash/month, one consistent currency | 140 |

Covered volume is 48 updates/month. Net time improvement is eight minutes/update, or 6.4 gross hours/month. After two support hours, the scenario releases **4.4 hours/month**. This is forecast capacity, not financial savings. The fictional timing notes cover only six updates and do not establish a representative baseline.

## What changes the recommendation

Change checking/correction to 20 minutes. The scenario now adds 5.2 net hours/month of work. The recommendation becomes **Revisit the approach**. A proposal could still pursue a different benefit, but it must explain and evaluate that benefit instead of retaining the unsupported time-saving claim.

The original scenario still needs permission review. It does not jump from a positive forecast to an authorized rollout. A material confidentiality problem or unsupported customer commitment would justify pausing even if the timing improved.

## What Jamie can claim now

Identified the issue, mapped the workflow, compared opportunities, developed a bounded proposal, identified affected people and collaborators, and designed an evaluation. Jamie cannot yet claim approval, implementation, saved cash, improved customer outcomes, or a promotion. Later results and shared contributions can be added with evidence and confirmation.
