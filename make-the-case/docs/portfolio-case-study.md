# Make the Case: from employee insight to a credible work-improvement decision

**Creator:** Rachel Wilde / Tomboj. **Status:** Working prototype, version 0.1. **Audience:** People who know the work and leaders deciding how to support improvements.

## The problem

Employees face requests to use AI while navigating uncertain expectations, limited time, data constraints, and the need to show credible value. An idea generator or a polished proposal can leave the difficult decisions unresolved: what changes, who is affected, what evidence exists, who needs to help, and what should happen next?

## Rachel's role

Rachel defined the opportunity through an extended exploration of AI change management, supplied prior operating-structure work as reference, challenged a narrow pitch-generator framing, and established the public newsletter as the experiment and networking channel. AI assisted research, synthesis, implementation, and checks. This project does not establish unmeasured reader or business outcomes.

## Consequential design choices

1. **Start with knowledge of work.** A title or technical qualification does not establish the value of an idea. An originator can contribute insight, evidence, coordination, or adoption while others build.
2. **Preserve the whole opportunity collection.** Selecting one focus does not discard other ideas. Context, decisions, and contributions travel in an exportable record.
3. **Make the evidence change the recommendation.** Calculations include checking, correction, coverage, uptake, and support. Negative capacity changes remain visible. Missing permissions and baseline information remain unresolved conditions.
4. **Separate AI reasoning from calculations and records.** An assistant can interview, challenge, and develop the case. The browser computes explicit scenarios and retains records; it makes no model calls, approval decisions, or company-system writes.
5. **Link a small task to an operating framework.** The exercise connects company goals, employee insight, people impacts, governance, resources, measurement, and ongoing ownership without requiring a reader to deploy an enterprise program.

## Demonstration

The fictional Northstar coordinator selects client-update preparation from three retained ideas. The default assumptions yield 4.4 potential net capacity hours/month. Increasing review time to 20 minutes changes the estimate to negative 5.2 hours and prompts reconsideration. Neither scenario grants permission to run a live test. Readers can inspect the calculation and requested next decision.

## Evidence and limits

The prototype includes deterministic checks and a portable example; the validation record distinguishes automated and browser checks from actual reader outcomes. No live AI is embedded, no customer records are connected, and no promotion, job-protection, or financial-result claim is made. Assistant portability is a design goal rather than completed comparative testing.

## Public learning and networking

Automation Thursday gives readers a practical task, GitHub supplies the reusable method, and the portfolio shows decisions and evidence. Reported use, questions, relevant leader conversations, and reuse can inform future work. A separate external test group is not required before publication.

The wider question is how employee insight becomes supported, governed work with measurable outcomes across a company. This project supplies a concrete opening for that conversation.
